Method

A score you can take apart.

No machine learning decides the verdict. The message is cleaned, matched against a learned keyword lexicon, described by four features, fuzzified, evaluated against nineteen rules, aggregated and defuzzified into one number — and every step is visible.

  1. 01

    Clean and extract

    Entities are decoded, scripts and tags removed, and only the visible body is analysed.

  2. 02

    Learned lexicon

    Weighted matches against the learned English and Serbian word lists produce the 0–10 keyword score.

  3. 03

    Measure

    Keywords, unique links, capital letters and punctuation are counted from the body.

  4. 04

    Fuzzify

    Each feature gets a degree of membership in triangular and trapezoidal sets.

  5. 05

    Infer

    Nineteen Mamdani rules combine memberships with min and max; each fired rule clips its output set.

  6. 06

    Defuzzify and explain

    The clipped sets are aggregated with max; the centroid 0–100 becomes the score, and the category is read from the same value.

What is measured

Only the message body is scored. The From and Subject fields are shown for context and never reach the classifier.

Signal How it is measured Scale
Keywords Weighted hits against the selected learned lexicon, summed and capped at 10. 0–10
Links Unique URLs found in the text or in href attributes, capped at 10. 0–10
Capitals Uppercase letters divided by all letters, after links are removed. 0–100%
Punctuation Exclamation and question marks divided by non-space characters, times 100. 0–100%

All six output sets, drawn from the membership functions used in the score.

Where the neural network fits

The MLP is trained offline on labelled SpamAssassin. Its only runtime product is the keyword lexicon — the weights saved in the two learned word lists.

It never sees the four features and never outputs the spam score. The verdict stays with the fuzzy rules.

Model Accuracy F1
Fuzzy + EN lexicon 90.3% 0.697
Fuzzy + both lexicons 90.0% 0.704
MLP standalone, EN 93.5% 0.770
MLP standalone, both 92.9% 0.736

Held-out SpamAssassin test: 750 emails, 125 spam. The Serbian corpus is partly synthetic, so those figures are not production accuracy.

Rules and limits

Nineteen rules are grouped by severity: 4 legitimate, 11 suspicious and 4 spam. Each rule clips its output set at its activation strength; the largest value at each point survives aggregation.

  • The four features are email-shaped, so SMS and chat messages are scored poorly.
  • Different emails can share a score when the fuzzy sets cannot separate them.
  • Membership boundaries were calibrated on the hand-labelled examples; all reported metrics come from held-out data.
  • The Serbian benchmark is partly machine-translated and partly synthetic.
